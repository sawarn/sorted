Four ways to build a transaction intelligence app

A diagnosis of the brief, four meaningfully different directions described screen by screen, and a recommendation for launch.

Diagnosis

The product's real asset is not the total — it is the audit trail behind the total. Sorted knows which messages it read, which it could not parse, what it excluded, and what it is unsure about. No bank-linked app can show that, because they are handed clean data and have nothing to explain. So the design problem is not “how do we visualise spending” — it is how do we make explanation the primary interaction.

Recommendation

Launch on Vault as the shipping product, and borrow exactly one move from Ledger Field: the month header.

Vault is the only direction that satisfies the north star — total to transaction list in two taps — without asking the user to learn a new visual language first. It is a feed, so the app is useful on day one with three parsed messages and still useful with three thousand. It is also the cheapest to build well on Android, which matters when the hard engineering is in the SMS parser, not the UI.

Ledger Field is the more distinctive product and the better story, but it is a second-release bet: the constellation only reads as intelligence once there is enough parsed history for the nodes to mean something, and a first-run user with an empty month sees an empty sky. Bring its month header into Vault now — the big explainable total with the excluded-and-unsure line attached to it — and keep the field itself as a candidate for the Insights “Month Story” view, where an expressive visual has earned its place.

Confetti is the right light mode for Vault, not a separate direction — take its color coding and playfulness and apply them to Vault's structure. Terminal is a power-user setting inside Vault (a density toggle and a compact row), not a product of its own.

The one-line version
Ship Vault's structure, Ledger Field's month header, Confetti's light mode, Terminal's density toggle.
